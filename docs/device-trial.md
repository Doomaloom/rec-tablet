# Device acceptance trial

The software POC is tested at 800 × 480, 1024 × 600, and 1280 × 800 with Fyne's software test renderer. This verifies layout and interaction without proving physical touch or GPU behavior. Complete these steps on the chosen Linux device and waterproof enclosure before a lesson trial.

1. Build and install the Go Linux archive for the device's architecture, with the required OpenGL/X11 libraries. Verify normal and fullscreen launch, readable font rendering, and the export dialog. Confirm startup shows the login keypad. Hold **Clear** on the login keypad or **My Classes** for three seconds, choose the **Display** profile that matches the device, and confirm the preference survives a restart. The profile is a device setting; Rec Tablet does not select it from the attached monitor or window dimensions. Confirm physical control sizes at the display's actual DPI.
2. In the large profile, verify the two-column class grid, scheduled-date calendar, three-option lesson menu, and two-column attendance pages on the 1024 × 600 screen. Check class changes across pages, month navigation, selected-date highlighting, a partial last attendance page, and the long swimmer name. The large profile has twelve attendance cards per page and touch targets of at least 48 logical pixels in the software layout. The private-class workflow remains on its current design; a separate private-class layout is deferred.
3. Start SPA Device Exports sharing with synthetic class data, then log in using the instructor’s six-digit code. Test leading zeroes, an invalid code, ended sharing, and a disconnected server. Confirm failures stay on login and can be retried. After a successful login, disable networking. Open each class, toggle swimmers present or absent in Attendance, and confirm an older lesson marks unchecked active swimmers absent when it opens. Choose a normal skill in Skills, then choose a swimmer and tap the completed subskills. Confirm the checklist stays open after the last subskill; save with the green checkmark rail button. Repeat until three saved successful attempts complete a skill; verify feedback at 0/3, 1/3, 2/3, and completion. Also open each timed benchmark, select multiple swimmers, enter laps, start and reset the heat timer, count laps by tapping swimmer names, and verify save records completed and incomplete times in Feedback.
4. Leave a subskill checklist unfinished, save it with the green checkmark, and verify the attempt appears in Feedback without increasing successful progress. Start another attempt and discard it with the red X. Hold a checked skill for two seconds and confirm its newest successful check and observation are removed. Review an older attempt through Feedback and confirm corrections still change earned progress only after submission.
5. On My Classes, test Cancel and Escape before confirming Log out. Confirm the keypad returns, and a different instructor cannot see the first instructor’s work. Re-login as the first instructor and check attendance, saved assessments, and drafts. With synthetic data, interrupt device power after a completed save and verify the keypad appears after boot; re-login with sharing active to verify database integrity and saved work. The automated suite tests abrupt process exit; real storage and power-loss behavior needs the hardware check.
6. Publish a reordered roster with stable IDs, then log out and log in with its new share code and confirm that all results still belong to the correct swimmer. Export and inspect the snapshot on a separate computer.
7. Try the actual enclosure and input device with wet hands and representative water exposure. Check accidental touches, button activation, paging, visibility, and recovery from a mistaken mark. Software cannot establish waterproofing or reliable submerged touch response.
8. Run for a full representative teaching shift on battery. Check startup time, heat, readability in pool lighting, touch latency, and endurance. Record the exact hardware, OS, screen resolution/DPI, Fyne version, enclosure, and results before considering the wearable trial passed.

Actual hardware selection, waterproofing, wet-touch reliability, battery endurance, and automatic data transfer remain outside the completed software checks.
